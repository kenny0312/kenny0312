## Q1. What architectures do Sierra, Decagon, Parloa, PolyAI, Intercom Fin and Chinese peers (晓多 XPT, 乐言, 阿里云 通义晓蜜, 扣子 Coze, 网易七鱼) publicly describe for making agents reliable? (Includes cross-cutting patterns: hallucination guardrails, memory, human handoff, observability and cost.)

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 consensus at the top US vendors is a **hybrid**. Business procedures are written in natural language and compiled into, or run inside, a deterministic scaffold (Decagon AOPs, Fin Procedures, Sierra Journeys/Agent SDK, Rasa Flows, Parlant Journeys). Hard policy is enforced by code, not prompts. Many specialized models work per turn: a "constellation" of retrievers, rerankers, classifiers and supervisors around a generator. Parallel "supervisor" models plus deterministic output guards act as guardrails. Every change is gated by simulation before release. Chinese e-commerce vendors (晓多, 乐言, 七鱼, 通义晓蜜) describe the same idea in different words: 大小模型协同 (big/small model collaboration), 快/慢思考 (fast/slow thinking) routing, SOP or workflow orchestration, and 人工兜底 (human fallback). The new 2026 trend is **vertical post-trained models** (Fin Apex, PolyAI Raven, Decagon on open models) replacing frontier APIs for the main answering step.

- **Memory: Agent Data Platform (ADP), Sierra Summit 2025.** ADP joins structured CDP and internal-system data with real-time conversational context across sessions and channels. **Cross-cutting: guardrails / hallucination mitigation**
- OpenAI's guide (2025) frames guardrails as **layered defense**: a relevance classifier, a safety classifier (jailbreak and injection), a PII filter, moderation, **tool safeguards** rated by risk, rules-based checks (regex, blocklists) and output validation. ### Inferences
- The shared architecture is **"LLM for understanding and language; code for decisions and numbers; models checking models; simulation before release."** Interview framing: the generator is one node in a graph, and reliability comes from the graph.
- 2026 split: vendors with scale (Fin, PolyAI, Decagon) are **post-training vertical models** on their own conversation logs, using outcome rewards and RFT with simulators. A Jekka "in-house Jekka-Markov model" claim fits that trend (see Q5).
- Chinese e-commerce vendors' "fast/slow" and "small model standard flow" designs are effectively **routing plus FSM for high-frequency intents** (shipping, returns, size questions). In e-commerce chat a few intents dominate volume, so deterministic fast paths cover much of it cheaply.

## Q2. What evaluation methods are best practice, and what key published results exist (τ-bench, τ²-bench, pass^k, simulated users, regression suites, LLM-as-judge)?

### Takeaway
Best practice in 2025–2026 has four parts. (1) **Simulated users** run against a sandboxed tool and database environment. (2) Results are scored on **final state or outcome**, not response text, where possible. (3) Agents are run k times and reported as **pass^k** (all k trials succeed), not pass@1. (4) **Regression and simulation gates in CI** are complemented by always-on production monitors and calibrated LLM judges. τ-bench showed GPT-4o-class agents at <25% pass^8 in retail (2024). By 2026 τ²-bench text domains are near-saturated at the top (~99% telecom pass^1). The frontier has moved to pass^k reliability and **voice** (τ-Voice: 26–51% for voice agents vs 85% for text GPT-5).

### Cited Findings
- **[Foundational] τ-bench (Sierra, June 2024; ICLR 2025)**: retail and airline domains, an LLM user simulator, and a domain policy document. Success = the final DB state matches the annotated goal state. It introduced **pass^k = the probability that all k i.i.d. trials succeed**. "State-of-the-art function calling agents (like gpt-4o) succeed on < 50% of the tasks, and are quite inconsistent (pass^8 < 25% in retail)" — [τ-bench arXiv 2406.12045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045); [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/1b126cc38b8638e07bef37e7b2bb72bf-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/tau-bench-shaping-development-evaluation-agents)
- Early leaderboard example: Claude 3.5 Sonnet airline pass^1 0.460 → pass^4 0.225, and retail 0.692 → 0.462 — [steel.dev registry](https://leaderboard.steel.dev/registry/benchmarks/tau-bench)
- Failure taxonomy (secondary, unverified against the paper): wrong arguments or information 55.5%, policy-violating decisions 25%, partially resolved compound requests 22.2% — [alphaXiv τ-bench page](https://alphaxiv.org/benchmarks/sierra/tau-bench)
- **τ²-bench (June 2025, arXiv 2506.07982)**: adds a **telecom** domain with **dual control**, where the user must also act on their own device state, so the agent has to *guide* the user. A "no-user" mode isolates reasoning. GPT-4.1 pass^1 drops from 74% (retail) and 56% (airline) to 34% (telecom). gpt-4.1-mini, o4-mini and claude-3.7-sonnet score about 50% on telecom. Pass^k declines faster with k on telecom than airline. ### Inferences
- In an interview, pass^k is the bridge to Jekka's founder language: "reliability = worst-case stability and **variance control**" is pass^k thinking (see Q5). If per-trial success is p, pass^k ≈ p^k for independent trials, so 95% per conversation gives only about 77% at k=5. Cutting variance (determinism, constrained outputs, code for decisions) raises pass^k faster than raising mean capability.
- A credible regression suite for e-commerce CS combines several parts. (a) A scripted simulation per intent × data state (order unpaid, shipped, delivered, refunded; in or out of return window). (b) Replayed production transcripts with mocked platform APIs. (c) A state-based assertion on order and refund DB changes. (d) A calibrated LLM judge only for tone and helpfulness. (e) pass^k over 5–8 seeds, gating deploys.
- The τ²-bench "dual-control" finding matters for e-commerce: guiding a buyer through actions in the Shopee app (cancel, upload a photo, confirm receipt) is exactly the harder mode.

## Q3. What latency budgets and stack choices are typical for voice agents in 2025–2026 (end-to-end response targets, streaming ASR/TTS, speech-to-speech models), and what are the main failure modes?

### Takeaway
The production default for enterprise customer service is still **cascaded** streaming ASR → LLM → streaming TTS, because transcripts, policy checks, component swapping and tool reliability matter (Sierra explicitly prioritizes this). The target is about **500–800 ms** from the user's end of speech to the agent's first audio, with turn detection and LLM time-to-first-token the biggest levers. Speech-to-speech (OpenAI gpt-realtime, Step-Audio 2, Qwen realtime, PolyAI "end-to-end" mode) cuts latency but costs debuggability, voice choice and tool accuracy. τ-Voice shows a large voice-vs-text capability gap. Main failure modes: premature cutoffs and false barge-in (echo, noise), misheard digits and alphanumerics, 8 kHz telephony audio, silent stalls, and tool latency.

### Cited Findings
- **Budget targets**: about 800 ms total round trip (VAD + STT + LLM + TTS + network), with STT under 300 ms ideal and under 150 ms achievable — [Introl 2025](https://introl.com/blog/voice-ai-infrastructure-real-time-speech-agents-asr-tts-guide-2025). One per-stage example sums to about 450 ms p50 *before* network and telephony: endpointing about 80 ms, final ASR about 120 ms, LLM first token about 180 ms, TTS first audio about 70 ms — [Fora Soft LiveKit 2026 playbook](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/livekit-ai-agents-guide). Turn detection typically takes 200–800 ms (optimized 200–400 ms), with silence thresholds of about 400 ms for quick Q&A up to about 800 ms for complex queries — [Hamming AI](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-ai-latency-whats-fast-whats-slow-how-to-fix-it). The commonly cited cascaded budget is 500–800 ms with overlapped streaming stages — [manthanguptaa primer](https://manthanguptaa.in/posts/voice_agents_primer/)
- **Sierra's voice engineering**: each turn has three latency-critical hops: end-of-speech detection, runtime reasoning, and synthesis. Sierra **trained a custom VAD** for noisy, multi-speaker audio that cuts reaction lag "by hundreds of milliseconds". It rebuilt reasoning as a **concurrent graph** (abuse detection, retrieval and API calls in parallel, synchronized only on dependencies). It **precomputes the likely next step**. It can merge steps with voice-to-voice models, but enterprise work prioritizes reliability, capability and observability — [Sierra: Engineering low-latency voice agents](https://sierra.ai/blog/voice-latency). Transcription uses **ensembles**: two ASR models in parallel, because one is strong on thick UK accents but hallucinates in silence, with conditional logic choosing between them — [ZenML summary](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-production-scale-voice-and-multi-modal-customer-experience-agents)
- **Turn detection models**: LiveKit's newer **audio-based** turn detector combines semantics with prosody (intonation, pitch, rhythm). Defaults drop to min/max endpointing delays of 0.3 s/2.5 s (vs 0.5 s/3.0 s) when it is active — [LiveKit docs: turn detector](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/turn-detector/); [Turns overview](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/). The older open-weights model is **text/semantic** — [HF livekit/turn-detector](https://huggingface.co/livekit/turn-detector). Pipecat **Smart Turn** works on audio directly (a summary describes a Whisper-Tiny encoder with a classifier, about 8M parameters, int8, BSD-2) — [smart-turn-livekit PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/smart-turn-livekit/0.3.0/); [LiveKit community](https://community.livekit.io/t/livekit-plugin-for-smart-turn-eot-detection-models/2013). A vendor benchmark (unverified) reports premature-cutoff rates of Smart Turn v3 45.3%, LiveKit local 18.0% and voxturn 16.7% — [voxturn model card](https://huggingface.co/AustinXiao/voxturn). **Deepgram Flux (GA Oct 2025)**: "conversational speech recognition" with model-integrated end-of-turn detection (about 260 ms) that removes the separate VAD — [Softcery](https://softcery.com/lab/how-to-choose-stt-tts-for-ai-voice-agents-in-2025-a-comprehensive-guide)
- **TTS time-to-first-audio is disputed**: vendors claim ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 at about 75 ms (model inference only, excluding network) and Cartesia Sonic at sub-90 ms (state-space model; older Sonic Turbo claims of about 40 ms). A single independent one-day read (Coval, Sep 8, 2026) measured median *perceived* TTFA of ElevenLabs Flash 185 ms, Gradium 214 ms and Cartesia Sonic 3.6 440 ms — [FutureAGI comparison](https://futureagi.com/blog/elevenlabs-vs-cartesia-tts-2026/); [Gradium](https://gradium.ai/content/best-low-latency-tts-apis-2026). Deepgram Nova-3 is listed at about 150 ms and $0.0043/min (Dec 2025 guide) — [Introl](https://introl.com/blog/voice-ai-infrastructure-real-time-speech-agents-asr-tts-guide-2025)
- **Speech-to-speech vs cascaded**: S2S collapses three hops into one (about 800 ms vs 1.0–1.5 s chained, per one blog). The costs: it is harder to debug, tool-call accuracy trails tool-tuned text models, and voices are limited to presets. Chained stacks keep transcripts, policy checks and component swaps — [Fora Soft OpenAI Realtime guide](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/openai-realtime-api-voice-agent-production-guide-2026); [Softcery real-time vs turn-based](https://softcery.com/lab/ai-voice-agents-real-time-vs-turn-based-tts-stt-architecture). Cost estimates conflict: about 10× the chained cost vs about $0.05–0.15/min after caching — same sources. OpenAI Realtime supports WebRTC, WebSocket and **SIP** (via a SIP trunk, with webhooks for incoming calls); SIP has limited routing and recording — [Fora Soft SIP/WebRTC](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/openai-realtime-api-webrtc-sip-websockets-integration). Model naming (gpt-realtime-1.5 / 2.1) is unverified. Full-Duplex-Bench-v3 compares GPT-Realtime with a cascaded Whisper→GPT-4o→TTS setup on tool use under disfluency (results not retrieved) — [arXiv 2604.04847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04847)
- **Chinese voice stacks**: Volcano Engine's 对话式AI (2024) is RTC plus Doubao ASR/LLM/TTS configured via OpenAPI, with edge access. It claims overall response latency "低至1s" (as low as 1 s), interruption at any time, and **双讲** (double-talk) handling via traditional AEC plus deep learning. It remains a cascaded pipeline — [火山引擎技术解析](https://www.volcengine.com/docs/6360/1330208); [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/0/787/365.htm). Alibaba 百炼's real-time voice (Qwen) is an end-to-end real-time voice model for customer service and assistants that supports **Function Calling** and three turn-detection modes (acoustic VAD, **semantic turn detection**, manual) — [阿里云百炼文档](https://help.aliyun.com/zh/model-studio/qwen-audio-realtime-user-guides). StepFun **Step-Audio 2** (2025, open) is an end-to-end audio LM with **voice-native tool calling** (e.g., web search to reduce hallucination) — [HF Step-Audio-2-mini](https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai/Step-Audio-2-mini); [BAAI tech report page](https://hub.baai.ac.cn/paper/b0521fa8-1beb-47f2-a284-aad2fb6d8683)
- **Failure modes**: AEC failure is the main cause of false barge-in and self-interruption loops — [Picovoice](https://picovoice.ai/guide/voice-agents/common-failure-modes/). Production targets: overlap under 200 ms and a false barge-in rate under 5%. Other failures include transcription hallucination, silent stalls, premature cutoffs and cross-call audio leaks — [Hamming interruption runbook](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-interruption-handling-runbook); [tenx](https://www.tenx.studio/blog/voice-ai-failure-modes). Most enterprise voice still runs on **8 kHz G.711** narrowband, which strips acoustic detail, and denoisers can hurt on PSTN — [Kore.ai](https://www.kore.ai/blog/voice-ai-challenges-and-how-to-fix); [Medium voice AI landscape](https://medium.com/@mjgmario/voice-ai-t1-the-landscape-frameworks-and-metrics-for-voice-ai-07b693f36d97). A single misheard digit in ID or payment flows derails the workflow — [Kore.ai](https://www.kore.ai/blog/voice-ai-challenges-and-how-to-fix). In τ-Voice, *agent-side* speech intelligibility was a non-issue (100% intelligible across 91 sampled sims); the gap lies in understanding and coordination — [ZenML τ-voice summary](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/benchmarking-real-time-voice-agents-on-real-world-customer-service-tasks)

### Inferences
- A defensible 2026 design for Jekka-style voice (inbound and outbound e-commerce or after-sales):
  - Telephony goes through SIP/RTC into streaming ASR with a semantic plus acoustic end-of-turn model.
  - The same SOP/state controller used for text keeps policy and tools identical across channels.
  - TTS streams sentence by sentence, with a pre-synthesized cache for greetings and confirmations.
  - Tool calls overlap with a short spoken acknowledgement ("let me check your order").
  - Order numbers and phone numbers are confirmed by readback or DTMF.
  - Budget target: under 800 ms p50 and under 1.5 s p95 to first audio.
- Chinese-market specifics likely matter: Mandarin plus dialects, very short utterances ("在吗", "发货没"), and outbound-call compliance. These favour semantic turn detection over pure silence thresholds.

### Gaps

- No independent latency benchmarks for Doubao, Qwen realtime or Step-Audio in customer-service settings.
- No hard data on Chinese telephony (运营商 carrier SIP trunks, 外呼 outbound-call regulations) found in this pass.

## Q5. What do Markov / state-machine controllers over LLMs look like in research and practice, and how might a "neural-network-like control structure with white-box debugging" plausibly be implemented? (Includes the labeled inference of Jekka's architecture and the candidate's transferable experience.)

### Takeaway
Research and practice converge on a few design choices. The LLM is placed inside an explicit **state machine or workflow graph**: states carry their own prompts, tools and allowed transitions; transitions are chosen by rules or by the LLM; pre- and post-decision controllers or verifiers check each step. Correctness is pushed into **constraints**: grammar-constrained decoding for format, assertions with backtracking for semantic rules, deterministic code for business decisions. A separate research line treats agent pipelines as **optimizable computational graphs** (GPTSwarm, TextGrad, DSPy), optimized with "textual gradients" or prompt or edge optimization. That is the most plausible concrete meaning of a "neural-network-like control structure with white-box debugging": a layered graph of small, typed, individually logged LLM or classifier "neurons" whose errors can be traced and corrected node by node.

### Cited Findings

**FSM / SOP-guided agents (research)**
- **[Foundational, Mar 2024; COLM 2024] StateFlow**: models LLM task-solving as a state machine. Each state runs specific prompts and tools, and transitions are decided by heuristic rules or by the LLM. It reports +13% / +28% success vs ReAct on InterCode SQL and ALFWorld at 5× / 3× lower cost — [arXiv 2403.11322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11322)
- **SOP-Agent (Jan 2025)**: SOPs become **decision graphs** whose nodes are candidate actions. A navigator filters valid actions at each step — [arXiv 2501.09316](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09316)
- **FlowAgent (Feb 2025)**: a **Procedure Description Language (PDL)** mixing natural language and pseudocode, with API nodes and ANSWER nodes. **Pre-decision controllers** guide choices and **post-decision controllers** check proposed actions. It claims compliance plus flexibility on out-of-workflow queries. Workflows are hand-built — [arXiv 2502.14345](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14345); [GitHub](https://github.com/Lightblues/FlowAgent)
- **Agent-S (Mar 2025)**: a "State Decision LLM" picks the next action from a predefined SOP workflow plus execution memory — [arXiv 2503.15520](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15520)
- **Compile, Then Page (Jul 2026)**: compiles SOPs into executable pseudo-code programs instead of hand-designed FSM states — [arXiv 2607.11346](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11346). **PolicyGuide (Aug 2026)**: persisted workflow state plus a separate verifier that flags unmet steps. It reports telecom Pass^4 of 0.675 vs FlowAgent's 0.350 — [arXiv 2608.19861](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19861). (The arXiv IDs were not independently checked.)
- Practice equivalents: Rasa CALM (LLM → commands → deterministic FlowPolicy); Parlant (guideline matching plus journeys); Fin Procedures (code snippets plus branches plus checkpoints); Decagon AOPs (NL compiled to logic) — see Q1 sources.

**Constraints, assertions, constrained decoding**
- **[Foundational, Dec 2023] DSPy Assertions**: `Assert`/`Suggest` constraints. On failure the pipeline **backtracks** and retries the module with the failed output and error message in context. Constraints pass up to 164% more often, with up to 37% more higher-quality responses — [arXiv 2312.13382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13382)
- **XGrammar (Nov 2024)**: grammar-constrained decoding with under 40 µs per-token mask generation, up to 3× faster on JSON Schema and 100×+ on CFG vs baselines — [arXiv 2411.15100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15100). **XGrammar-2 (Jan 2026)**: on BFCL-v3, schema validity reaches 100% for all Llama models tested, and constrained Llama-3.2-3B beat unconstrained Llama-3.1-70B — [arXiv 2601.04426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04426). Its own blog warns it enforces **format, not semantics** — [MLC blog 2026-05-04](https://blog.mlc.ai/2026/05/04/xgrammar-2-fast-customizable-structured-generation). Reported as the default backend in vLLM and SGLang (secondary) — [tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-16-grammar-constrained-generation-output-reliability). JSONSchemaBench shows capabilities vary by framework and schema — [arXiv 2501.10868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10868v2)

**Agents as optimizable graphs (the "neural-network-like" analogue)**
- **[Foundational, ICML 2024] GPTSwarm**: agents as computational graphs. Nodes do LLM inference or tool use, and edges carry information. A **node optimizer** refines prompts and an **edge optimizer** prunes or creates connections via learned probabilities — [PMLR v235](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/zhuge24a.html); [arXiv 2402.16823](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16823v3)
- **[Foundational, Jun 2024] TextGrad**: a compound AI system is turned into a computation graph. LLM natural-language critiques act as "gradients" that are **back-propagated** to revise each component, with a PyTorch-like API. Example: GPT-4o zero-shot on Google-Proof QA rose from 51% to 55% — [arXiv 2406.07496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07496)

### Inferences

**INFERENCE — what Jekka's architecture plausibly looks like (no source describes Jekka internals; everything in this subsection is speculation grounded in the public claims above and in industry patterns)**
1. **"Jekka-Markov" = an explicit Markov/state-machine dialogue controller.** Most plausibly each merchant SOP (pre-sale sizing, order status, return/refund, logistics complaint, coupon) is compiled into states with per-state prompts, allowed tools and allowed transitions. The next state depends only on the current state plus a structured belief (slots: order_id, order_status, within_return_window, sentiment…), which is the literal Markov property. Transitions are picked by a classifier or LLM, constrained to the legal set (as in StateFlow, FlowAgent and Rasa CALM). It could also be a fine-tuned model ("in-house Jekka-Markov model") trained to emit state transitions and actions rather than free text.
2. **"Neural-network-like control structure" = a layered graph of small "neurons".** Likely each node is a narrow, typed LLM or classifier call: intent, slot extraction, policy check, retrieval, response planning, response rendering, verification. Each has defined inputs and outputs, wired in layers, possibly with gating weights or thresholds (cf. GPTSwarm nodes and edges, Sierra's constellation and concurrent graph). "Generalization" comes from LLM nodes, and "white-box" comes from the fact that every node's input and output is logged and human-readable.
3. **"White-box debugging" = per-node traces plus credit assignment.** When a conversation fails, engineers or an automated critic locate the node whose output was wrong (wrong intent? wrong slot? wrong tool result? wrong rendering?) and fix that node's prompt, examples or training data. This is backprop by analogy, as TextGrad and DSPy optimizers formalise. A Decagon-style Trace View and Parlant-style guideline-match tracing are the industry analogues.
4. **"Variance control" = pass^k engineering.** Plausible levers: low temperature; constrained or structured decoding at every node; deterministic code for eligibility, price and date maths; canned or templated responses for high-risk intents (as in Rasa's human-authored responses); self-consistency or multi-sample voting at decision nodes; verifier or supervisor nodes; regression suites measured on worst-case and pass^k rather than mean.
5. **"Heisenberg: 32-bit floating-point, zero hallucinations"** is marketing language. A plausible technical reading is that **numeric and factual fields (prices, discounts, stock, delivery dates, order amounts) are never generated by the LLM**. They are computed exactly by code from platform APIs and slotted into responses, possibly with a final grounding verifier that rejects any unsupported claim. "Zero hallucination" in practice likely means *closed-domain answers are restricted to retrieved or API facts with fallback or handoff*, not a literal guarantee.
6. **Voice reuse**: the Voice AI Agent likely shares the same controller, with a cascaded ASR/TTS front end (most consistent with "white-box" and SOP adherence), possibly using Chinese realtime models for latency.
7. **Integration layer**: DouDou is likely a platform-adapter layer (Shopee chat API as an authorized Shopee CB service-market vendor; Shopify app or widget; 抖店/有赞 via ISV routes) feeding one controller. Response-rate KPIs are a primary value metric.
